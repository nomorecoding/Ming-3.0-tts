Featured Abilities

Getting a line of speech right is rarely just a matter of reading the words. Which voice is this character? How should the line land? Where does it pause, laugh or sigh? Is it still the same character in another language or dialect? Today those answers are scattered across tools: hunt for a reference clip that is close enough, nudge emotion and pace, splice a laugh in during post, then re-check pronunciation and timbre every time the language changes. Ming-3.0-tts brings voice, delivery, non-verbal sounds, language, dialect and attribute controls into one speech-generation framework.

  • 🎨 Start from the character, not from a clip. No reference recording that sounds right? Describe the voice: age, texture, temperament, role. Voice Design renders it with no audio at all. Or pick one of 100+ built-in Chinese voices and four English ones, or clone from a short reference. On InstructTTSEval-ZH, tiny ranks #2 among the models compared below on average ACC (74.30%), APS (83.80%) and DSD (80.50%).
  • 🎬 Direct the line in plain words. "Like telling an old friend about the past: warm, close, a little nostalgic." Freeform takes a sentence of direction instead of a panel of acoustic knobs. Voice Design decides who speaks; Freeform decides how the line is said.
  • 💬 Laughs and sighs belong in the script. Fourteen inline tags cover breath, cough, sigh, laughter, hesitation, surprise, and question and confirmation particles. Write them where they happen, in Chinese or English, and the model performs them in the speaker's own voice instead of leaving them for post. On NVBench, the two variants are SOTA on all six single-tag metrics (Chinese and English CER, PCER, OCER), and tiny is SOTA on all three English multi-tag metrics.
  • 🌐 One character, seven languages, two dialects. Chinese, English, German, Spanish, French, Korean and Japanese, plus Sichuanese and Cantonese, without rebuilding the voice for each one. SOTA German (0.325%) and Spanish (0.725%) WER (tiny); SOTA Sichuanese CER and dialect accuracy on both WSC-Eval-TTS sets; SOTA Cantonese CER (6.15%) and dialect accuracy (98.00%) on WSYue-TTS-eval-Base (0.5b).
  • 🎚️ Control when it matters. For announcements, narration and batch jobs, choose supported emotion, speed, pitch and volume settings for repeatable control. 0.5b is SOTA on average attribute accuracy (93.30%), pitch accuracy (86.00%) and WER (0.317%), and #2 on speed (99.00%). On CV3 emotion, tiny is SOTA on text-related Happy (100%) and Sad (88%) and 0.5b on Angry (94%); tiny's 74.30% average ranks #2.
  • 🎙️ A whole conversation, not stitched lines. Give tiny one short reference per speaker and a speaker_1: / speaker_2: script, and it performs the dialogue in one pass, turn-taking included. SOTA 1.85% CER on the podcast benchmark (tiny).

Two variants: Ming-3.0-tts-0.5b, dense and built on Qwen2.5-0.5B; and Ming-3.0-tts-tiny, an 8B-A1B mixture of experts with 1B active, built on Ling V3. Both cover every ability above except podcast, which is tiny only; both are benchmarked side by side in the repository README.

Under the Hood: One Sequence, Many Controls

Target language, Freeform direction, paralinguistic tags and structured controls enter the model as ordinary tokens next to the text. These cues share the model's prompt format. The public APIs expose task-specific inputs, shown in the Cookbook.
