Featured Abilities

Getting a line of speech right is rarely just a matter of reading the words. Which voice is this character? How should the line land? Where does it pause, laugh or sigh? Which language is it, or which dialect? Today those answers are scattered across tools: hunt for a reference clip that is close enough, nudge emotion and pace, splice a laugh in during post, then re-check pronunciation and timbre every time the language changes. Ming-3.0-tts brings voice, delivery, non-verbal sounds, language, dialect and attribute controls into one speech-generation framework. Each generation is one task; the controls below are not combined in a single call.

  • 🎨 Start from the character, not from a clip. No reference recording that sounds right? Describe the voice: age, texture, temperament, role. Voice Design renders it with no audio at all. Or pick one of 479 built-in Chinese voices and five English ones, also with no audio. Or clone from a short reference. These are three separate calls: a description, a built-in name and a reference clip are not combined, and none of these calls also takes emotion, dialect, speed or a freeform instruction. On InstructTTSEval-ZH, tiny ranks #2 among the models compared below on average ACC (74.30%), APS (83.80%) and DSD (80.50%).
  • 🎬 Direct the line in plain words. "Like telling an old friend about the past: warm, close, a little nostalgic." Freeform is its own task: one instruction, not a voice-design description and not a built-in voice. On 0.5b the instruction can follow a reference clip or, with no reference, choose the voice; tiny always needs the reference clip. It does not take emotion, dialect, speed, pitch, volume or inline tags.
  • 💬 Laughs and sighs belong in the script. Fourteen inline tags cover breath, cough, sigh, laughter, hesitation, surprise, and question and confirmation particles. They belong to Chinese or English cloning: written where they happen, performed in that reference speaker's voice. They are not accepted on voice design, built-in voices, dialect, emotion, attribute control, or the five new languages. On NVBench, the two variants are SOTA on all six single-tag metrics (Chinese and English CER, PCER, OCER), and tiny is SOTA on all three English multi-tag metrics.
  • 🌐 Seven languages on a cloned voice; dialects on their own call. Chinese and English use the cloning task; German, Spanish, French, Korean and Japanese use the multilingual task. One language per call, from a reference clip. Sichuanese and Cantonese are a separate dialect task on a reference voice, with no target-language setting, and that call does not take emotion, speed or tags. SOTA German (0.325%) and Spanish (0.725%) WER (tiny); SOTA Sichuanese CER and dialect accuracy on both WSC-Eval-TTS sets; SOTA Cantonese CER (6.15%) and dialect accuracy (98.00%) on WSYue-TTS-eval-Base (0.5b).
  • 🎚️ Control when it matters. Emotion is one task (高兴, 悲伤, 愤怒, 恐惧, 厌恶, 惊讶, 中性). Speed, pitch and volume are a different task and are set together (慢速 / 中速 / 快速, pitch and volume 低 / 中 / 高). Neither task takes a target language, a dialect, tags, or a designed or built-in voice. 0.5b is SOTA on average attribute accuracy (93.30%), pitch accuracy (86.00%) and WER (0.317%), and #2 on speed (99.00%). On CV3 emotion, tiny is SOTA on text-related Happy (100%) and Sad (88%) and 0.5b on Angry (94%); tiny's 74.30% average ranks #2.
  • 🎙️ A whole conversation, not stitched lines. Give tiny one short reference per speaker and a speaker_1: / speaker_2: script, and it performs the dialogue in one pass, turn-taking included. Podcast does not take a target language, emotion, dialect or tags. SOTA 1.85% CER on the podcast benchmark (tiny).

Two variants: Ming-3.0-tts-0.5b, dense and built on Qwen2.5-0.5B; and Ming-3.0-tts-tiny, an 8B-A1B mixture of experts with 1B active, built on Ling V3. Both cover every ability above except podcast, which is tiny only; both are benchmarked side by side in the repository README.

Under the Hood: One Sequence, Many Controls

Target language, a freeform instruction, paralinguistic tags and structured controls share one prompt format: they are ordinary tokens next to the text. A single request fills the slots for one task. Voice design, a built-in voice, cloning, freeform, emotion, speed/pitch/volume, dialect, paralinguistic tags and podcast are not combined. The public APIs expose that split, shown in the Cookbook.
